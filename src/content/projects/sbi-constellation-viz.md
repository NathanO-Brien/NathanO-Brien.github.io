details: "Days after his second inauguration, President Trump signed an executive order calling for 'Golden Dome,' a defense initiative meant to protect the U.S. from a wide range of missile threats, including a SBI layer that would strike missiles during their boost phase before they could deploy countermeasures. The order left many structural design choices unspecified: which geographies to cover, what missile types to defend against, how large a salvo to handle. Those choices matter enormously. A constellation sized for a small regional threat looks nothing like one sized for a peer adversary, and the difference shows up directly in how many satellites you need and where you put them. This project intends to shed light on this reality using a set of starter assumptions and a transparent method. Instead of building one static analysis, this tool serves to generate and evaluate thousands of possible constellation designs in a simple, interactive form. It will be used at two expert workshops, one at the Geneva Centre for Security Policy and one at the American Enterprise Institute, in order to ground arms-control conversations in something more concrete than rhetoric. The specific numbers you might see in the tool are illustrative only; both the tool and its outputs are still evolving."

## Novelty

Prior SBI studies exist, but they universally assume a symmetric, Walker-like satellite constellation. Our approach breaks this assumption using a mixed-integer linear programming method first introduced by Professor Hang Woon Lee. Our approach is also novel in that we present the full end-to-end assumption set necessary to go from threat region and salvo size to constellation characteristics.
